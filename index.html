Rule set

Infinite deck, dealer stands on soft 17, blackjack pays 3:2, dealer peeks for blackjack with an ace or ten up, double on any first two cards, double after split allowed, one split per hand, one card to each split ace, no surrender, no insurance unless stated.

An infinite deck is how most RNG and crypto blackjack games deal, since every card comes from a full deck. It is also a close stand-in for a 6 or 8 deck shoe. The charts below match the published 4 to 8 deck chart for a dealer who stands on soft 17 with double after split in every cell but two. Soft 13 (A,2) against a 5 and soft 15 (A,4) against a 4 are hits with an infinite deck and doubles in a 4 to 8 deck shoe. Surrender is left out because this rule set does not offer it.

With these rules and perfect basic strategy the player loses 0.57% of the initial bet per round on average. WhyDuel's blackjack page describes a crypto casino original with these exact rules and puts the game math at about 0.57%, which lines up with the computed figure.

What each mistake costs

Cost is the drop in expected value when you make the wrong play instead of the right one, in percent of your initial bet, for that one hand. The last column is how much the overall house edge rises if you make that mistake every time the hand comes up. Both are computed for the rule set above, after the dealer has checked for blackjack.

The hand people argue about is nearly free. Standing on hard 16 against a 10 costs 0.06% of the bet. Hitting is right, but only just. Hitting hard 16 against a 6 is a different story at 27.72%.

The expensive mistakes are the ones that throw away a strong hand. Splitting 10s against a 10 costs 50.39% of the bet and comes up often, so doing it every time adds 1.355 points to the house edge on its own. Not doubling 11 against a 6 gives up 33.37%.

Insurance is a side bet with its own edge. It pays 2:1 on a 4 in 13 chance, so it loses 1/13 of the insurance stake. A half bet insurance costs 3.85% of the main bet every time a dealer ace shows. Even money on a blackjack is the same bet under another name.

The same game played five ways. Loss per 1,000 is in units of the initial bet over 1,000 rounds.

Never bust: stand on hard 12 or more, hit soft 17 or less6.08%60.8
Mimic the dealer: hit below 17, stand on 17 or more, no doubles or splits5.67%56.7

Copying the dealer keeps the 3:2 blackjack but gives up every double and split, and the player still acts first, so a player bust loses even when the dealer would have busted too. The result is 5.67% instead of 0.57%. Never busting is worse still at 6.08%, because standing on a stiff hand against a 7 or higher loses more often than hitting.

Method and checks

Computation. The script in code/ works out the dealer's final total for each upcard by recursion over an infinite deck, where a ten-value card has a 4 in 13 chance and every other rank 1 in 13. Player stand, hit and double values follow from that, and split values add up two one-card hands with doubling allowed and no resplit. Run python code/build_data.py to rebuild all three CSV files.

Check against published tables. Stand, hit and double values match the Wizard of Odds infinite deck expected return table to four decimals. Two examples are stand on 16 against a 10 at minus 0.5404 and double 11 against a 6 at 0.6674. Split values are a little lower here because that table allows resplitting to four hands, and its overall result is minus 0.51% against minus 0.57% here for the same reason.

Chart check. All cells but two match the Wizard of Odds 4 to 8 deck chart for a dealer who stands on soft 17 with double after split. The two exceptions are soft 13 against a 5 and soft 15 against a 4, where hitting beats doubling by 0.74% and 0.09% of the bet with an infinite deck. The same Wizard of Odds infinite deck table shows the same order for both hands, so the difference comes from deck count, not from the code.

Frequencies. The added edge column multiplies the cost by the chance of being dealt that exact two-card situation with that upcard and no dealer blackjack. Hands that reach the same total after a hit are not counted, so the column is a floor for hard totals.

Limits. These are long-run averages. They say nothing about one session, and they are not advice to play.
